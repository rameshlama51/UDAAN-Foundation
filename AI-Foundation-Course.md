# AI Foundation Course -new

## 1. Course Overview 

The AI Foundation Course introduces learners to the core concepts ..., methods, tools, and applications of Artificial Intelligence (AI). It builds a practical understanding of how AI systems represent information, learn from data, make predictions, and support decision-making.

The course combines conceptual foundations with hands-on exploration and problem-based learning (PBL). Learners will move from identifying real-world problems to selecting appropriate AI approaches, experimenting with data and models, and communicating evidence-based results.

## 2. Course Goals

By the end of the course, learners should be able to:

- Explain the meaning, scope, history, and major branches of AI.
- Distinguish AI, machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI.
- Describe how data, features, representations, models, training, and inference relate to one another.
- Recognize common AI tasks, including classification, regression, clustering, recommendation, and generation.
- Explain the basic workflow of an AI project, from problem formulation to evaluation and deployment.
- Explore datasets and perform introductory data analysis.
- Build or experiment with simple machine-learning models using appropriate tools.
- Evaluate model performance and identify limitations, bias, and risks.
- Formulate an AI-based response to a practical problem and communicate the supporting evidence.
- Formulate an AI agent

## 3. Target Learners and Prerequisites

**Target learners:** Beginners, students, educators, professionals, and other learners interested in understanding and applying AI.

**Prerequisites:** No prior AI experience is required. Basic computer literacy is expected. Familiarity with mathematics or programming is helpful but not mandatory; technical activities can be adjusted to learner readiness.

## 4. Learning Approach

The course uses a progressive, problem-based learning approach:

1. **Problem identification:** Identify a meaningful real-world problem and understand its context.
2. **Exploration:** Examine relevant data, concepts, stakeholders, and possible explanations.
3. **Problem formulation:** Translate the problem into a clear question or AI task.
4. **Method selection:** Determine whether AI is appropriate and identify a suitable approach.
5. **Experimentation:** Work with data, tools, and simple models.
6. **Evaluation:** Assess results using appropriate evidence and metrics.
7. **Communication and reflection:** Present findings, limitations, ethical considerations, and possible next steps.

Learners should engage with practical examples throughout the course rather than treating AI as a collection of disconnected algorithms.

## 5. Proposed Course Modules

### Module 1: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence

- What is intelligence? What is artificial intelligence?
- Brief history and evolution of AI
- AI, machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI
- Narrow AI and the idea of general AI
- Examples of AI in everyday life and different sectors

**Learning outcome:** Explain AI and identify common AI applications and terminology.

### Module 2: Data and Representation

- Data types: structured, semi-structured, and unstructured
- Text, images, audio, video, and sensor data
- Data quality, collection, labeling, and preparation
- Features, patterns, and representations
- Introduction to vectors, embeddings, and feature spaces

**Learning outcome:** Explain how data is prepared and represented for computational learning.

### Module 3: Foundations of Machine Learning

- What is machine learning?
- Supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning
- Classification and regression
- Clustering and dimensionality reduction
- Training, validation, testing, and inference
- Overfitting, underfitting, and generalization

**Learning outcome:** Distinguish major machine-learning paradigms and match basic tasks to suitable approaches.

### Module 4: Introduction to Deep Learning

- Neurons and artificial neural networks
- Layers, weights, biases, and activation functions
- Loss functions and optimization
- Introduction to backpropagation
- Convolutional neural networks and image applications
- Sequential models and time-series applications

**Learning outcome:** Describe the basic structure and learning process of a neural network.

### Module 5: Generative AI and Large Language Models

- Generative AI concepts and use cases
- Tokens, embeddings, and attention
- Transformer architecture at a conceptual level
- Large language models (LLMs) and prompting
- Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
- Limitations, hallucinations, and responsible use

**Learning outcome:** Explain how generative AI and LLM-based applications work at a foundational level.

### Module 6: AI Tools and Practical Experimentation

- Introduction to Python and notebooks, where appropriate
- Exploring and visualizing datasets
- Using accessible AI and machine-learning tools
- Running a simple model or guided AI experiment
- Interpreting outputs and documenting experiments

**Learning outcome:** Complete a guided AI experiment and explain its inputs, process, outputs, and limitations.

### Module 7: AI Ethics, Safety, and Responsible AI

- Fairness, bias, and representation
- Privacy, consent, and data protection
- Transparency and explainability
- Reliability, security, and human oversight
- Social and environmental impacts
- Responsible AI practices

**Learning outcome:** Identify key ethical risks and propose practical safeguards for an AI application.

### Module 8: Problem-Based AI Project

- Select and understand a real-world problem
- Define users, context, constraints, and success criteria
- Identify data requirements and potential sources
- Formulate the task and select a suitable method
- Build or demonstrate a small prototype
- Evaluate results and document limitations
- Present findings and recommendations

**Learning outcome:** Present an evidence-informed AI project that connects a defined problem to an appropriate method and evaluation.

## 6. Assessment Strategy

Assessment should emphasize understanding, experimentation, and problem-solving.

- **Concept checks:** Short quizzes and explanations of key concepts.
- **Practical exercises:** Data exploration and guided AI experiments.
- **Learning reflections:** Short records of questions, decisions, and lessons learned.
- **Problem formulation:** Assessment of the clarity and relevance of the selected problem.
- **Final project:** Evaluation of the problem definition, method selection, implementation or demonstration, evidence, ethical considerations, and presentation.

## 7. Learning Resources and Tools

The final toolset should reflect learner access, course duration, and available infrastructure. Possible resources include:

- Python and Jupyter notebooks for practical work.
- NumPy, pandas, and Matplotlib for introductory data work.
- scikit-learn for foundational machine-learning experiments.
- TensorFlow or PyTorch for optional deep-learning activities.
- Accessible generative AI tools for guided exploration.

Tools support learning objectives; they should not replace conceptual understanding or critical evaluation.

## 8. Expected Course Outputs

By the end of the course, learners should have:

- A foundational AI concept and terminology reference.
- Completed exercises in data exploration and AI experimentation.
- A documented real-world problem formulation.
- A small AI prototype, demonstration, or evidence-based solution proposal.
- A final presentation explaining the approach, findings, limitations, and responsible-use considerations.

## 9. Items to Finalize During Curriculum Planning

The following decisions should be made with the course team before delivery:

- Total course duration and session schedule.
- Learner age group, background, and expected entry level.
- Balance between conceptual learning, coding, and no-code activities.
- Local examples and sector-specific problem scenarios.
- Required hardware, software, connectivity, and learning materials.
- Detailed session plans, exercises, and assessment rubrics.
